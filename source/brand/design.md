# Design guide

How we look, everywhere: the website, the decks, any image we produce. The AI reads this plus `tokens.css` before any visual decision. The setup skill personalizes it; after that it grows one rule at a time.

## Philosophy

Institutional but alive: a hospital-and-engineering research chair, not a startup. Confident, restrained, generous whitespace, one big scrollable home page. Inspired by owkin.com and whitelabgx.com — mostly light, clinical-clean sections, punctuated by a couple of full-bleed dark-blue blocks for emphasis rather than color everywhere. Every visual decision should be defensible against this paragraph.

## Tokens

`tokens.css` is the only place where colors, fonts, and the spacing scale are defined. Nothing in `site/` or in a deck hardcodes a hex value, a font name, or a magic pixel number. Adding a design idea that needs a new value means adding the token first, with a name that says what it's for.

- **Color**: the BOPA logo's four blues (`--bopa-blue-1..4`) are the source. `--color-accent` is the darkest (`--bopa-blue-1`, best contrast on white); `--color-dark` is a deeper navy used only for full-bleed emphasis sections (hero, one spotlight section per page — not every other section). Neutrals are cool-toned to sit with the blues: a light blue-gray `--color-bg`, deep navy `--color-ink` instead of pure black.
- **One narrow, deliberate exception to blue-only**: `--color-gold` (`#b8954b`) marks the "Médico-économique" category on `/innovations` only - its active filter pill, its category icon border/color, its label text. Added 2026-08-27, at the owner's explicit request, specifically to make that one category legible as financially/environmentally distinct from the clinical phases. It does not extend to the category's own project cards (MesGardes/HepatGreen stay navy blue like every other card) or anywhere else on the site - don't generalize this into a second accent color for other uses.
- A solid `--color-accent` fill on a dark navy section reads as neon, not premium. But buttons still need a visible contour there, not bare text either - the "Faire un don" CTA went from solid fill, to plain text-only, to its current form: `.btn-outline-premium` (dark translucent fill + white inset outline + white text), the same reusable class the hero buttons use. That's the standard treatment for a button on a dark section here.
- **Type**: still system fonts (no webfont chosen yet), but display headings use `font-weight: 700` and `--tracking-tight` (-0.02em) to read as confident and current rather than default browser serif. Revisit if the owner picks a webfont.
- On light sections, a short tagline/label defaulting to plain navy ink or gray reads as flat - the owner asked for the partners tagline to be "plus peps" (livelier) and in blue. Fix was `color: var(--color-accent)` (not `--color-ink`/`--color-muted`) plus a size bump on the tagline itself. When a short standalone line of text feels too quiet, brand blue plus a touch more size is the default fix to reach for before inventing a new treatment.
  That size bump then broke the phrase's wrap inside its narrow fixed-width column (`.partners-text`, 11rem) - "Ensemble, innovons sans limites" wrapped across 3 lines and looked messy, and the owner specifically wanted "innovons sans limites" kept together as one unit. Fixed by wrapping each half in its own `<span>` with a forced `<br />` between them (not relying on the natural wrap point, which drifts with column width/font changes) and setting `white-space: nowrap` on the whole line so a future width change can't silently re-wrap it. When forcing a specific line break in a heading/tagline, always verify with `scrollWidth` vs `clientWidth` that the intended one-line half doesn't itself overflow at the column width you land on.
  Getting the line break right didn't settle it, though - a two-tone color split on the two halves still read as flat to the owner ("still don't like it"), three separate color/size passes in. What actually landed: stop treating it as a type problem and give the block its own visual presence instead - a soft gradient backdrop (`linear-gradient(135deg, color-mix(... var(--color-accent) 12%, transparent), transparent 75%)`) plus a left accent bar, i.e. a highlighted callout rather than plain text next to a logo strip. If a repeated "make this text pop" request survives two or three color/size iterations without landing, that's a signal the fix isn't a text property at all - reach for a backdrop/border/card treatment on the container next, rather than a fourth type tweak. (Practical trap hit while doing this: adding `padding` to an element that already has a fixed `max-width` shrinks its *content* width by the padding amount under the default `border-box` sizing, which reintroduced the overflow this section just fixed - widen the `max-width` by roughly the new padding whenever you add padding to something with a pre-tuned width.)

## Type

- Display face and text face are both the system sans stack for now (`--font-display` / `--font-text` in tokens) — no webfont has been chosen. If the owner picks one, self-host it (put files in `site/public/fonts/`, declare with `font-display: optional`) so the site never flashes or jumps on load.
- One type scale, defined in tokens, used everywhere. Resist per-page font sizes.
- `--text-xl` is a `clamp()` and already scales down on narrow screens; `--text-2xl` (used by the base `h1` and `.stat-row .stat-value`) is a flat `3.25rem` with no floor. On a phone that left the page `h1` (innovations/boptech/publications - the homepage's own `.hero h1` already had its own clamp) *smaller* than `h2` (both bottoming out, but `h1`'s fixed value has no floor to hit while `h2`'s clamp does), and left the "Nos projets et réalisations" stat numbers (1.6M€, etc.) at a fixed 52px - roughly 14% of a 375px screen for one short string. Both are now `clamp(2rem, 6vw, var(--text-2xl))`, unchanged above ~866px wide. Any *other* place using `--text-2xl` directly needs the same check before it ships - a flat value on a large-scale token is a mobile bug waiting to be noticed.
  `--text-lg` (1.375rem, used by `.lede`) is flat too, and it's easy to assume a *medium* token is safe just because it isn't obviously huge like `--text-2xl` - it wasn't, until a section's opening block (kicker → h2 → lede → a plain body `<p>` right after) stacked three or four visibly different, non-scaling sizes in quick succession on a phone and read as "too big and too varied" even though each individual size looked reasonable in isolation. `.lede` now drops to `--text-base` under `max-width: 40rem`. The actual rule: check every token used for running text (not just the largest ones) against what sits directly above and below it at phone width - a cascade of unrelated sizes is as noticeable as any single oversized element, and less obvious to spot by looking at one class's CSS in isolation.
- **Typographic hierarchy, formalized 2026-08-27**: "Notre Mission"'s own H2 ("Améliorer la chirurgie, grâce au numérique" - `font-size: clamp(2rem, 3.1vw, 3.15rem); font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.08; letter-spacing: -0.035em;`) and its intro paragraph (`clamp(1rem, 1.3vw, 1.2rem); line-height: 1.55;`) are now the explicit **Niveau 1** (grands titres) and **Niveau 2** (leads/intros) étalons for the whole site, at the owner's request - not just a one-off match for one section. Currently applied to: Hero `h1` + lede, the context-band's 4 texts, the home's "Les innovations BOPA" H2, and `/innovations`' own H1. When a new grand titre or lead-level intro is added anywhere on the site, reuse these exact metrics rather than inventing new ones - don't apply Niveau 2 to every `<p>` though, only text that's editorially an introduction at that hierarchy level (a card description or footer line is Niveau 3/4, not 2).

## Layout and components

- Generous whitespace; when in doubt, remove an element instead of shrinking everything.
- Build a small set of components and reuse them. Keep an inventory here as they appear, with a pointer to where each lives. A new page should mostly assemble existing components.
  - `.section-block` (+ `.dark` variant): full-bleed section wrapper used to build the scrollable home page — see `site/src/styles/site.css`.
  - `.kicker`, `.lede`: small uppercase label above a heading, and a larger intro paragraph below it. `.lede` is sized for a short, punchy 1-2 sentence intro - it's not "the paragraph right under an h2" by default. The "Nous soutenir" paragraph (3 full sentences of running text) was styled `.lede` simply because of its position and read as oversized once it was actually read at that size; moved to a plain body-size class. Pick the class by the text's length/role, not by where it sits.
  - `.card-grid` / `.card`: simple bordered cards, used for the projects sections. Cards in the same row stretch to equal height on desktop by default (grid `align-items: stretch`) - no mobile override needed, `auto-fit` already collapses multi-card rows to one column there. When a card has a photo/video, make `.card` a flex column and give the media `margin-top: auto` so it lands flush at the bottom instead of leaving slack below it.
  - `.about-v2__person` / `.about-v2__person-photo`: current person cards for the team ("03" subsection of the "Qui sommes-nous" chapter, replacing the older `.team-member-card`/`.equipe-layout` two-column split as of 2026-08-27 - a light bordered card with a photo, not a dark navy circle-avatar card). Real headshots still work exactly as before: drop a file into `site/public/images/team-photos/` named after the person's slug (accents/titles stripped, lowercased, hyphenated - `slugify` in `index.astro`) and the card swaps its placeholder gradient for the real photo automatically; squares resized with sharp's `resize({width, height, fit: 'cover'})`, not left to CSS `object-fit` alone. The "always render the optional line, pin the trailing element to the bottom via flex + `margin-top: auto`" fix (first found on the old `.team-member-card`) had to be re-applied here from scratch when Axelle Piguet's missing `credentials` field shortened her card and floated her LinkedIn icon above the other three's - **a fix like this doesn't carry over automatically when a component gets rebuilt with new class names; re-derive it (or grep the old component's CSS for it) rather than assuming a rewrite already accounts for lessons baked into what it replaced.** Never add a LinkedIn icon without a real profile URL - same "no icon without a real URL" rule as before.
  - `.about-v2` (own `<style>` block in `index.astro`, `--mp-*` scope extended to include it in `site.css`): the "Qui sommes-nous" chapter (2026-08-27), placed right after "Nos projets phares" - 4 subsections (Chaire/recherche → environnement clinique du CHB, navy full-bleed with a real photo → équipe (`.about-v2__person`, `#equipe` anchor lives here now) → mécénat). Founders/foundations/patrons logos and BOpEx/BOPTECH links all reuse the project's existing real assets/URLs (see `source/brief.md`), never a pack's placeholder path or `href="#"`. Institutional content (01/03/04) sits directly on the page background, no enclosing card, per the "few boxes" rule below - only the team cards and the navy clinical block break that.
  - `.logo-grid`: partner logo wall, used by the partners section.
  - `.stat-row`: big number + label, used by the publications section.
  - `.nav-dropdown`: `<details>`-based dropdown menu, used by the two top-nav groups (La Chaire, Innovations).
  - Auto-advancing crossfade carousel (dots, pause on hover/focus, restart timer on manual navigation): not automatically the right tool for "N things of the same kind" - the team photo gallery copied this pattern at first and the owner rejected it (2026-08-25): full-width made a single photo feel oversized, and the small corner dots were too easy to miss to find as a navigation control at all. Swapped for a plain `.team-gallery-grid` (CSS grid, `auto-fit`/`minmax`) showing every photo at once, smaller, nothing to browse. The homepage's own project carousel (`#project-slider`) went the same way on 2026-08-27, for the same reason. Default to a static grid/row for "here are our things" content; reach for a carousel only when one-at-a-time reveal is the actual goal, not just because a similar feature exists elsewhere - this project has now rejected the carousel pattern twice.
  - `.context-band` / `.context-item`: the compact institutional banner directly under the Hero (`#environnement`) - icon + short two-line fact, 4 *strictly equal* columns on desktop (`repeat(4, minmax(0,1fr))` as of 2026-08-27; previously `1fr 1.45fr 1fr 1.15fr`, extra width quietly compensating for the CHB fragment's longer text), 2-col then 1-col on narrower widths, thin dividers between items. Answers "why is BOPA's environment good for innovation" (location, clinical proximity, access, co-construction rate) - deliberately excludes productivity numbers (publications/projects/thèses), which live further down (`#projets`, `#soutenir`).
    Each fragment's text is split into explicit `.context-line` spans (`display:block`, desktop `white-space:nowrap`) so every fragment shows *exactly* 2 lines on desktop rather than whatever the browser's natural wrap produces - the CHB fragment specifically needs this (its text is the longest of the four). `.context-text`'s font-size dropped below Mission's Niveau-2 lead scale for this same patch, at the owner's explicit request ("ne doit plus utiliser la même taille que le grand lead de Mission") - a reminder that a component-wide typographic rule (like the Niveau 1/2 étalons above) can still get a deliberate, later exception for one specific place; don't "fix" it back to the étalon without checking whether it was an intentional carve-out first.
    This exactly-2-lines promise only holds from ~78rem (1248px) viewport width up - below that, `.context-band-row` falls back to its existing 2-column/natural-wrap layout (moved from the old 56rem breakpoint to match, after measuring the CHB fragment still overflowing its column by 17px at 1150px). See the `vw`-decoupling lesson below for why a wider viewport doesn't just mean "more room."
    Dropping the forced `nowrap` had one side effect: "Centre Hépato-Biliaire" itself could now break between the two words on natural wrap, which read as a typo/formatting glitch rather than an institution name. Fixed with a small `.context-nowrap` span around just that name (not the whole line) - when relaxing a text-wrapping constraint, check whether any proper noun/compound term inside the now-wrappable text needs its own individual protection, rather than assuming "no more forced line control" means nothing can look broken.
    **Reversed again the same day**: the owner asked for the band to go full-viewport-width (`width:100vw; margin-inline:calc(50% - 50vw)`, escaping `.wrap`'s `max-width` entirely, so the 3 dividers land at 25/50/75% of the *screen* not the content column) and relaxed "exactly 2 lines" to "2-3 lines, legibility first" - dropped the forced `white-space:nowrap` on `.context-line` (now just a visual line-grouping hint, wraps normally) and raised the type ~25%. Since each item is now genuinely `25vw` of an *uncapped* container, a `vw`-based font clamp is safe here in a way it wasn't in the capped version - the decoupling bug in the entry below is specifically about a container that stops growing while the font doesn't; a full-bleed row never stops growing, so both scale together. The CHB fragment still needed its own measured breakpoint though (2-column fallback moved to 82rem: 3 lines at 1300px viewport, 4 at 1250px) - "full width" bought back a lot of room but the fragment's raw text length still eventually outgrows even 25vw of a normal laptop screen once the font is large enough to be legible.
  - `.bopa-vision-v4` (`--mp-*` scope, own `<style>` block in `index.astro`; class renamed from `.bopa-vision` in the V4 rebuild, 2026-08-27): "Notre vision" between "Nos innovations" and "Qui sommes-nous" - a 5-step timeline (Innovation → Acceptabilité → Interopérabilité → Soutenabilité → Progrès pour tous) on a connector line, inline SVG throughout (no external image assets; never swap these icons or generate new ones - the owner has repeated this instruction across every Vision pack). Pure copy/design component with no real-world facts to source, one of the simplest integrations this project has done.
    The connector (`.bopa-vision-v4__timeline::before`) was originally a dotted line (`repeating-linear-gradient`), matching Mission's own timeline exactly. Changed same day (2026-08-27, separate small patch) to a solid `--mp-blue-bright` line with a single chevron arrowhead (`::after`, border-top+border-right rotated 45deg) right before the "Progrès pour tous" endpoint - the owner wanted the line to read as directional (Innovation → destination) rather than five equal-looking waypoints, without touching Mission's own dotted style or anything else in Vision (sizes, circles, icons, text, background, spacing all explicitly out of scope for that patch). A useful template for narrow "surgical" patches generally: when a pack says "change only X, everything else must stay pixel-identical," verify the *other* metrics with computed-style checks too (not just the one thing that changed) - it's the fastest way to confirm nothing else drifted.
    **First attempt shipped invisible**: the arrow used the same `right: 4.5%` as the line itself (matching the line's own endpoint), which placed it directly underneath the "Progrès pour tous" icon circle - completely hidden, since the circle has an opaque background. `getComputedStyle` on the pseudo-element looked entirely correct (right color, right size, `display: block`, `opacity: .9`) and gave false confidence; the bug only surfaced when the owner looked at the live page. **Checking a computed style confirms an element's own properties are correct, not that it's actually visible** - for anything positioned near or under another opaque element, also compare bounding boxes (or coordinates derived from the container rect + the offset) to confirm there's no overlap, especially for absolutely-positioned decorative pseudo-elements that have no natural place in the layout flow to anchor them away from siblings.
    **Second attempt (fixed the hiding, created a new complaint)**: moving the arrow to `right: 14%` fixed the visibility, but left the *line* at its original `right: 4.5%` - so the line still ran 9.5 percentage points past the arrow before disappearing under the circle, reading as "the trajectory keeps going behind the arrowhead" rather than the arrow being the line's actual end. Fixed (2026-08-27, V2 correctif) by giving both pseudo-elements the *same* `right` value (15.5%, tuned once more) so the line terminates exactly where the arrow sits. When a decorative marker is meant to read as "the end of X," check that X itself actually stops there too - a marker placed correctly in isolation can still look wrong if the thing it's marking keeps going past it.
    Landing the arrow-to-circle gap in the owner's requested 30-45px range hit the same fixed-circle-vs-percentage-container tension as the context-band patch: a pure `%` offset can't sit at a constant *pixel* distance from a fixed-size circle across every viewport width (measured 28px at 1000px vs 51px at 1920px for the same 15.5%). Since the failure mode here is "gap slightly outside the requested range" rather than "content overflows/hides" (unlike the context-band case), the fix was simpler - just target the middle of the requested range at a representative width and accept mild drift at the extremes, rather than reaching for a `calc(% + rem)` hybrid. Worth distinguishing when a hybrid formula (precise, more complex) is worth it versus a single tuned value (simpler, "close enough"): reach for the hybrid when missing the target means broken/overflowing content, accept a plain value when missing it only means a little more or less breathing room than asked.
    Went through three visibly different visual identities in one day before landing here: its own exact pale-blue background (`--color-vision-bg`, `#f1f6fd`, tokens.css - kept defined even though unused now, in case it's wanted again) → same navy as Notre Mission (`var(--mp-navy)`, owner's request after seeing it live) → V4's full metric harmonization with Mission (owner's request again, after judging V3's 166px circles and 4rem+ H2 "too big and too airy" next to Mission). Each time the pack's own stated direction was the *starting point*, not a constraint that survives the owner actually looking at the live result - don't resist a follow-up request to redo a section already integrated faithfully once, if that's what she asks for next.
    **The V4 harmonization surfaced a real discovery, not just a copy-the-CSS exercise**: `.bopa-mission-v2`'s own component CSS specifies `padding: clamp(4.75rem, 7vw, 7rem) 2rem clamp(5.25rem, 8vw, 7.5rem)`, but Mission's *actual* computed padding is a flat `64px`/`64px` (`var(--space-5)` top and bottom) - a `#notre-mission, #innovations-bopa { padding-top/bottom: var(--space-5) }` id rule (a deliberate "these sections should flow directly into each other" tightening, not a bug - see the design.md junction-padding convention) has been silently beating the component's own clamp the whole time. Trying to hand-replicate Mission's clamp() formula on Vision would have made them *look* close but drift apart at in-between viewport widths, since Vision's clamp wouldn't have been overridden the same way. The actual fix: add `#notre-vision` to that same id rule, so both sections get the identical fixed `--space-5` value at every screen width - **when a pack asks you to match another component's "current" metrics exactly, always check the computed style of the reference element itself, not just its own class's source CSS - a sibling rule can be quietly overriding it**, the same root cause as the whole "id beats class" bug family, just discovered this time through a metrics-matching request rather than a visual glitch.

Before renaming any id: `grep` the *old* id across every stylesheet and every page (not just the one being edited - `innovations.astro` had its own `href="/#contact"` link that needed updating too), fix every hit, then grep the *new* id afterward to confirm nothing else already claims it. Doing this thoroughly here caught zero leftover stray rules (they'd already been cleaned in an earlier pass) - the point is the check itself, not assuming a clean result.

Pair with a README in `source/brand/assets/<name>/` telling the owner where to drop their own originals. **Always wrap that `readdirSync` in try/catch** (as `team-photos`' own reader already did) - the team gallery's own reader didn't, and removing its last two photos (2026-08-27, owner's request) deleted the folder itself along with them, which would have thrown `ENOENT` and crashed the build/dev server had the gap not been caught before publishing. Any folder a page reads at build time can end up not existing, not just empty - handle both.
  - Any number quoted on one page that's really a count of content defined on another page (e.g. the homepage hero's "projets innovants" stat vs. the actual project list on `/innovations`) belongs in a shared module under `site/src/data/` (see `projects.js`), imported by both pages, with the number computed (`.reduce`/`.length`), never hand-typed on the second page. A hand-typed copy *will* drift the next time a project is added or removed on the other page - this one was already wrong (said 15, real count was 14) before anyone noticed.
  - A page's own `<h1>` can silently drift from the label the nav uses to link to it - the innovations page's nav entry (`Base.astro`) already said "Nos projets" while its own `<h1>` still said "Nos innovations", unnoticed until the owner pointed it out. When touching a page's `<h1>`/`<title>`, cross-check it against how `nav` in `Base.astro` labels the link that points there.
  - **When replacing a section with an externally-sourced redesign** (this project's now-established pattern - see `source/brief.md`): grep both `site.css` and the page's own `<style>` block for any `#that-section-id` selector before assuming the new component's own class-based styles will just apply. A previous integration pass's id-based override (background, padding, or a `#section h2`/`.lede`/`.kicker` descendant rule) **beats a class selector regardless of source order or which file loads later** - this silently won back the old flat color/spacing/text-color over the new component twice in a row (2026-08-27, both the "BOPA V2" homepage pass and the "Mission/Projets V3" pass that replaced part of it one day later), and the only way to catch it is to check computed styles against the reference *after* integrating, not just read the new CSS and assume it wins. When a new redesign is deliberately scoped under its own class/token namespace (as both of these were, precisely to avoid this), keep old id-based rules for that same section id from lingering - remove or narrow them, don't just add the new component alongside them.
    A fourth variant of this same root cause (2026-08-27, a later same-day corrective pass): the context band under the Hero had *two full parallel implementations* of `.context-band`/`.context-band-row`/`.context-item` coexisting - an original hand-built flex-based one still living in `index.astro`'s own `<style>` block (using `border-left`), and the newer grid-based one added to `site.css` during the "V2" pass (using `border-right`). Nobody had ever deleted the first one when the second was added, so both applied simultaneously, doubling every separator - invisible from reading either file in isolation, only found by grepping the *same class name* across every file that could define it. When a pack's CSS "replaces" a component that already has bespoke CSS somewhere in the project, actively search for and delete every old definition of that component (not just add the new one) - check `site.css` *and* every page's own scoped `<style>`, not just the one file you're editing.
    A fifth variant (2026-08-27, the "Qui sommes-nous" chapter rebuild): moving a section to a new position in the page can leave *position-dependent* rules stranded on its old id/neighbor. `#qui-sommes-nous`/`#equipe` had their own id-scoped padding overrides *and* `#qui-sommes-nous` alone carried the page's only `.fade-to-navy` dark-transition treatment (needed because it used to sit directly before the dark "Nous soutenir" section) - reusing the same ids for the new chapter (now positioned earlier, no longer adjacent to "Nous soutenir") would have silently applied stale padding/fade rules never designed for the new content or position. Fixed by deleting the stale id rules entirely (the new component manages its own vertical rhythm) and moving `.fade-to-navy` to `#approche`, which became the new last-light-section-before-soutenir. When reordering or merging sections, grep for the old id in *every* stylesheet and ask what depended on its *position* (transitions to a neighbor, junction-tightening padding), not just what depended on its *content*.
    A sixth variant, and the most consequential yet (2026-08-27, the "Notre approche" removal pass): `site.css` carried a whole legacy layer from the original "BOPA V2 flat backgrounds" pass - bare `#qui-sommes-nous { background: var(--color-navy); color: ... }`, `#soutenir { background: var(--color-bg-tint); color: ... }`, plus `#qui-sommes-nous h2/h3/p { color: #fff / near-white }` - that had silently survived *both* the "Qui sommes-nous" and "Nous soutenir" rebuilds. Because these are bare ID selectors (not compound with a class, unlike the already-known `#qui-sommes-nous.fade-to-navy`/`#soutenir.dark` variants that *were* checked and correctly found inert), they kept beating `.about-v2`/`.bopa-support`'s own class-based background and text colors the entire time - the live site had been rendering the whole "Qui sommes-nous" chapter as one solid navy block with white headings (instead of the intended light/dark alternation) and "Nous soutenir" with a light background (instead of navy) since each section's own launch commit, undetected until this later, unrelated cleanup pass happened to grep the same ids for another reason. Neither rebuild's own verification had checked the section's own background-color or heading color computed style - only child elements, layout, and asset URLs were checked each time. **The lesson generalizes past this specific bug family: after building or replacing a component under its own class name, explicitly check the *outer wrapping element's* computed background-color and the color of its top-level headings/paragraphs, not just the internal pieces you just wrote** - a wrapper-level override is the easiest thing to miss because every child you actually look at can still be individually correct.
  - Don't paste a redesign pack's own absolute pixel/rem targets for a "slightly bigger" adjustment without checking they were baselined against *this project's current state* first - a pack's suggested Hero type sizes assumed a starting point that had already drifted from what's actually live (`.hero h1`'s min clamp value, specifically). Measure the actual current computed size in the browser, compute the requested percentage from that, and use the result - don't trust an absolute number handed over from outside the project, even when it looks close.
  - **Reusing a component across pages means reusing its custom-property scope too, not just its class name.** `.bopa-project-card` (site.css) reads `--mp-navy-deep`/`--mp-blue-bright`, which were only defined inside `.bopa-mission-v2, .bopa-projects-v2 { ... }` (the homepage sections that introduced it) - when `/innovations` reused the class for its own project grid (2026-08-27), the card rendered with a silently transparent background, because a CSS custom property has no value at all outside the scope that defines it (no cross-scope fallback, unlike inheritance). Fixed by adding the new page's own top-level class to that same definition list. Before reusing any `.bopa-*`/component class on a new page, check whether its styles reference a custom property, and if so, extend that property's defining selector to include the new page - don't assume the class alone carries everything it needs.
  - `.innovations-v2` (own `<style>` block in `innovations.astro`, importing categories/projects from `site/src/data/projects.js` rather than hardcoding them): the "Toutes nos innovations" catalogue - journey strip, working tab filters (vanilla JS, `[data-project-filter]`/`[hidden]`, no reload), then one row per category (icon + label + tagline aside, hairline-separated, no boxed frames) with `.bopa-project-card` vignettes reused verbatim from the homepage. Same "same exact component, more projects" instruction as the mission/projets pair above - don't fork a near-identical card style for a new page's project grid; extend the existing one's scope instead (see the custom-property note just above).
    On this page specifically (not the homepage), cards are deliberately non-clickable `<article>`s with no arrow - the owner's 2026-08-27 explicit call, since most of the 14 projects have no real destination yet and a whole-card link or a decorative arrow implies one. Only two projects get an explicit small inline text CTA instead (`.innovations-v2__card-action`, own class, not `.bopa-project-card__arrow`): LiverSight's video and the Compte-rendu card's Uncovr link, both real, both opening in a new tab. The homepage's own `.bopa-project-card` instances are unaffected (still real links with the arrow) - the two pages intentionally diverge in interaction model while sharing the same visual card. Don't add a whole-card link back on `/innovations` just because a project later gets a real detail page - ask first, since "no dead-end click" was itself an explicit requirement here.
- Motion is subtle and purposeful, or absent. Never bouncy by default.

## Imagery

- Originals live in `source/brand/assets/` at full resolution; the site serves processed copies only (see the images section of `source/formats/website.md`). Python/PIL and ImageMagick are not available in this environment (see the PNG-transparency note below) - for the actual resize/re-encode/EXIF-strip step, use the `sharp` package already installed in `site/node_modules` (it's an Astro dependency): `node -e "require('sharp')('source/path.png').rotate().resize({width:2000,withoutEnlargement:true}).jpeg({quality:82}).toFile('site/public/path.jpg')"` - `rotate()` with no args auto-orients from EXIF, and `.toFile()` strips metadata by default.
  Typing an exact escape sequence or special character (a non-breaking hyphen `‑`, a regex Unicode range like `̀-ͯ`) directly into a source-file edit doesn't reliably land as the literal backslash-u text - it tends to come out as the already-decoded character instead, which looks identical in a diff summary but is a different (and for a regex, non-functional) set of bytes. Verify with `od -c` or Node's `.codePointAt()` after writing. If it comes out wrong, build the exact bytes deliberately instead of retyping: `printf` with explicit escapes (`\xNN` for raw UTF-8 bytes, or `\\u0300` with the backslash doubled) into a scratch file, then splice that file's content into the target via a small Node script - don't just keep re-editing the same spot hoping it lands differently.
- Photography for everything that can be photographed - the real team, real equipment, real procedures, not stock photos or staged compositions. Diagrams/schematics are fine specifically for explaining how a technical mechanism works (e.g. InMachina's "comment ça marche" schema, the "progrès" bridge diagram for the vision section) - that's a different job than illustrating a person, place, or object, and a photo can't do it. This flip-flopped twice already on InMachina (schema removed for a reused photo, then restored) - don't re-remove a diagram without asking.
  Same logic extends to stock-style illustrative images (Henri's AI-mascot artwork, Goldfinger's touchless-3D-model render, the Hepatgreen/coût financier/coût environnemental illustrations): fine for a project or concept that has no real BOPA photo opportunity yet (in-development tools, abstract economic/environmental framing, an AI assistant with no physical form) - not a general license to use stock photography once a real photo becomes possible.
  Hard exception, no flexibility: never put a stock/generic photo under a real named person's name (team cards, bios) - that's not "no photo opportunity yet", it's fabricating what a specific real individual looks like. Show initials in a plain avatar instead until a real headshot exists (see `.team-member-avatar`), and don't add a LinkedIn/social icon for someone without a real URL to point it at. The owner has since supplied real LinkedIn URLs for all four current team members (`.team-member-linkedin`, also recorded in `source/content/equipe.md`) - the rule was never "no icons ever", just "no icon without a real URL"; add one for a new person only once they give you their actual profile link.
- Checking whether a PNG actually has transparent pixels (not just RGBA format, which says nothing about whether any pixel's alpha is below 255): Python/PIL and ImageMagick are not available in this environment (only non-functional Windows Store stub aliases). Instead, load the image in the Browser tool via `javascript_tool`, draw it to a `<canvas>`, and read `getImageData()` - sample the four corners plus center for a quick check, or scan every pixel and report the opaque/transparent/partial split for a thorough one (see the `progres.png` and homepage-gradient investigation, confirmed genuinely transparent this way on 2026-08-25). This also proved the file was fine when the owner reported it "not background-less" - matched the existing caching-related "verify live, don't assume regression" pattern once again.
  That same diagram later had a real, different problem: the owner wanted its built-in "fondu" (fade) removed - the diagram's own artwork faded toward its right edge via a genuine alpha gradient (sampled: alpha 129 and 57 at two points well inside the artwork, not just edge antialiasing). Fixed by reading the raw pixel buffer with `sharp(...).raw().toBuffer({resolveWithObject:true})`, thresholding every 4th byte (the alpha channel) to 0 or 255, and re-encoding with `sharp(data, {raw:{width,height,channels}}).png().toFile(...)` - this makes anything with *any* visible content fully opaque while leaving true-transparent background alone. It does *not* fix fading caused by pale/light RGB color choices baked into the art itself (confirmed here: the right side stayed visually washed-out after the alpha fix, because those pixels were always a light blue, just now at full opacity) - that's a recolor, not a transparency fix, and isn't something to attempt without asking, since the "correct" un-faded color was never stored anywhere to recover.
  Outcome: the owner didn't want this trade-off - called the alpha-thresholded result "floue" (blurry, likely the loss of antialiasing at the fixed-alpha edges reading as lower quality) and asked to revert to the original file entirely, fade and all. Reverted with `git show <commit-before-the-edit>:<path> > <path>` for both the `source/brand/assets/` original and the `site/public/` served copy. Don't re-apply this alpha-threshold technique to this file again without asking first - it was tried and explicitly rejected once already.

## Responsiveness, learned the hard way

- A hover-lift/scale effect on a card inside a horizontally-scrolling row (`overflow-x: auto`, e.g. `.innovations-v2__cards`) gets silently clipped unless the row itself has enough padding: per spec, setting one overflow axis to anything but `visible` forces the *other* axis to compute as `auto` too, even if never set - so a row with `overflow-x: auto` and zero top/bottom padding clips vertically exactly where a lifted card's rest-state edge already sits (confirmed 2026-08-27: clearance measured 0px on all 14 `/innovations` cards before the fix). Give that row enough top/bottom padding to cover the transform distance plus the hover shadow's blur bleed (not just the transform) - and don't add it to a container that shares a grid row with something else (like a category label sitting beside it), since padding shifts the row's content relative to that sibling; verify actual clearance with `getBoundingClientRect()` (card top minus row top), not by eyeballing the CSS.
  Pair any hover-lift with `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) { ... }` around the `:hover` rule - without it, the effect can trigger (or get stuck on) a touch tap, which has no real "unhover" to end it. Verify with `window.matchMedia('(hover: hover)').matches` at each target width/device, not just visually.
- Every visual change gets checked at mobile width (~390px) and a normal desktop before publishing, and at ~2560px if the audience uses large screens.
- When adapting to large screens, scale whole modules together (type, dimensions, spacing, wraps). Scaling only headings leaves a small design floating in whitespace.
- Late CSS wins ties: media-query override blocks go at the end of the style block they override, or they silently lose the cascade. This has caused real bugs three times now (a mobile hero width, a mobile nav button that overflowed its menu, then the mobile nav *dropdown panels themselves* - `@media (max-width: 76rem) { .nav-dropdown-panel { visibility: visible; ... } }` sat before the unconditional base `.nav-dropdown-panel { visibility: hidden; }` in `site.css`, so the base rule always won and the mobile menu's dropdowns never actually opened, on any phone, until someone opened one and looked). That third case broke functionality, not just looks - a layout glitch is easy to spot scrolling past it, a menu that silently never opens is not, unless someone actually taps it. When adding *any* mobile/responsive override for a selector that already has an unconditional rule elsewhere, place the `@media` block immediately after that rule (not wherever feels natural in the file), and verify the computed style at the target width, not just that the code looks right.
  Verifying this one is itself tricky in this environment: reading `getComputedStyle` immediately (or even after a `setTimeout`) on a property that's mid-`transition` can report stale values when the Browser pane isn't actually displayed/compositing frames (a real, separate limitation seen throughout this project - screenshots fail with "the page is not compositing frames" when the pane is backgrounded). Don't conclude a transition-driven show/hide is broken from a hidden-tab computed-style read alone; either front the tab first, or test a state reached via `transition: none` (as the mobile nav override already sets) where the computed value can't lag behind the class change.
  Same underlying limitation showed up a different way (2026-08-27, "Notre vision" pass): after using `resize_window` with `preset: "mobile"` then `"desktop"` earlier in a session, a later `preset: "desktop"` call left `window.innerWidth`/`innerHeight` reading `0` and every responsive check computed against the *mobile* breakpoint despite the preset claiming to restore desktop - because the pane wasn't actually displayed at that moment, so it had no real size to "return to." Explicit pixel dimensions (`resize_window` with `width`/`height`, e.g. 1440×900) forced a real viewport and immediately fixed it. If a responsive computed-style check ever comes back for the wrong breakpoint, suspect the pane's display state before suspecting the CSS - re-check with explicit width/height rather than a preset.
- **A `min(Xvw, Yrem)` container width and a `clamp(min, Xvw, max)` font-size inside it can silently decouple** once the viewport gets narrow enough that the font hits its floor: the font stops shrinking, but the `vw`-based container keeps shrinking with it, so the available width per line keeps dropping while the text doesn't get any smaller to compensate. Hit this 2026-08-27 harmonizing the Hero lede to Notre Mission's type scale: a `.nowrap`-locked compound word ("hépato-bilio-pancréatique") that fit on 3 lines at both ~700px and ~1440px viewports still wrapped to a 4th line specifically in the ~1024px range between them, because that's exactly where the font sits at its `1rem` floor but the `min(vw, rem)` column width is still shrinking. A pure `vw` ratio increase doesn't fix this (verified: pushing the ratio right up to the container's own outer limit still wasn't enough at 1024px) - the fix is a `max(Nrem, min(Xvw, Yrem))` floor, an absolute minimum width that holds through the floored-font range, layered under the existing vw/rem clamp for wider screens. When a pack asks to reuse another element's exact type scale, don't just test the width-sensitive result at the two ends of the responsive range (narrowest and widest) - the floor-crossover point in the *middle* is where this kind of thing breaks, and it won't show up unless you specifically check the width where the font's own clamp bottoms out.
  The same decoupling bites in the *opposite* direction too, and is worse to catch because "wider screen = more room" feels intuitively safe. `.context-band`'s items live inside `.section-block .wrap`, which is capped at `var(--max-width)` (74rem) - past ~1232px viewport, the wrap (and every item inside its grid) stops growing at all, width frozen. But `.context-text` still used a `clamp(min, Xvw, max)` font-size keyed to raw viewport width, so past that same point the *font* kept growing toward its ceiling while the *column* had already stopped - confirmed 2026-08-27: the CHB fragment fit fine at 1260px but overflowed its own column by 52px at 1920px, despite 1920px being "more room." Once a container's width is capped independent of the viewport (a `min(vw, rem)` or a `max-width` token, not just "the whole page"), any font-size inside it needs the same cap logic (a flat value, or a `clamp` capped to the *container's* plateau point, never raw `vw` alone) - check the widest realistic viewport explicitly, not just "wide enough that it must be fine."
- Never put a standard CSS property and its `-webkit-`-prefixed twin (`backdrop-filter`, `mask-image`, etc.) as two declarations in the *same* rule - the production build's CSS minifier treats them as redundant duplicates and silently drops one (whichever came second in source), breaking whichever browser needed the dropped one. Put the `-webkit-` version in its own rule instead, guarded by `@supports not (<property>: <value>)`. This is a build-output bug, invisible in the dev server and in the source - the only way to catch it is to grep the actual files in `dist/` after `npx astro build`.
- Same "works in dev, breaks in the real build" trap for Node frontmatter code: don't resolve a filesystem path with `new URL('../relative/path', import.meta.url)` in a page's frontmatter - the production build bundles that file into `dist/.prerender/chunks/`, so the relative path resolves against the wrong location and throws `ENOENT` at build time even though `astro dev` never showed a problem. Use `path.join(process.cwd(), ...)` instead (stable in both modes, since `cwd` is always the `site/` root when running `astro dev`/`astro build` from there). Any page doing filesystem work (like the team gallery's `readdirSync`) needs an actual `npx astro build` run before shipping, not just a dev-server check.
- `.wrap`'s max-width/centering is defined as `.section-block .wrap` (site.css) - a descendant selector, not a standalone class. An element placed *between* two sections (not nested inside either one - e.g. a card straddling a section boundary with a negative margin) gets `class="wrap"` but silently stretches to the full viewport width, since the scoped rule never matches it. Give it its own explicit `max-width: var(--max-width); margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;` (plus a `calc(100% - 2 * var(--space-4))` width so it still keeps edge padding below the max-width breakpoint) instead of assuming `.wrap` alone is enough.
- Adjacent `.section-block`s stack their padding-top + padding-bottom into a gap that reads as too big (default `--space-6` top and bottom = 16rem combined). Check every new section junction for this; the fix already used several times is `padding-bottom: var(--space-5)` on the section above and `padding-top: var(--space-5)` on the one below.
  This had only ever been fixed junction-by-junction as each one got touched for some other reason - by 2026-08-25 that left several *older* junctions still at the untouched default (`#projets` → `#equipe` and `#equipe` → `#soutenir` were both still 256px combined, worse than the ones already "fixed"). When the owner says spacing feels too big "in general" rather than pointing at one spot, measure every section's computed `padding-top`/`padding-bottom` on the live page and tighten every junction to the `space-5`/`space-5` standard in one pass, not just the one that prompted the complaint.
  Same over-stacking can happen *within* one section too, not just between two: the projects page's intro (kicker/h1/lede) → `.phases-image` → first `.section-sub` had two separate `margin-top: var(--space-5)` gaps back to back (one before the diagram, one after) - each looked fine alone but together, plus the diagram's own height, read as too much empty space before any project actually appeared. Tightened both to `var(--space-4)`. When several elements in a row each carry their own top margin, judge the *sum* against the page, not each margin in isolation.
  `.section-sub` is a page-scoped class redeclared separately in both `index.astro` and `innovations.astro` (Astro doesn't share page-scoped styles - see the component inventory note above). Tightening it in one page's `<style>` block does nothing for the other's identically-named copy: `innovations.astro`'s got fixed first, and `index.astro`'s sat at the untouched `var(--space-6)` default for a full extra session before the owner reported the same "too much blank space" complaint a third time and it turned out to be this. When a spacing fix applies to a class name that exists on more than one page, grep for every page-scoped redeclaration of it and check each one, not just the page you're currently looking at.
- A `vw`-based width tuned for desktop (to track some other element's own `vw` sizing) silently caps that same element at a fraction of the screen on mobile too, with no visual warning - it just quietly shrinks. Anything sized in `vw` needs an explicit check at ~375px, not just at desktop widths; the hero's text column once capped itself at 50vw on a phone (half the screen, for no reason - there was no second column to leave room for).
- No split screens or side-by-side columns on phones - single column, full width, always. When adding a new two-column layout, its mobile collapse is not optional and needs verifying at ~375px before publishing, not assumed from the desktop code.
- Same idea one level down: a flex item with no explicit width (`.stat-row .stat` - just three items in a `flex-wrap` row) looks fine on desktop where there's room, but on a phone `flex-wrap` gives each item only as much width as its own content needs, which isn't enough for a label like "Mécénat collecté depuis 2020" to fit on one line. Any flex/grid item holding text that must stay on one line needs an explicit mobile width (`flex: 1 1 100%` here, stacking the three vertically), not just "let it wrap and hope."
- One session surfaced six separate mobile bugs at once (nav menu, gradient anchors, heading hierarchy, stat sizing, pipeline text-align, and the lede size-cascade above) - each was real and each was found only by actually loading the page at ~375px and checking, not by reading the desktop CSS. None of them were visible from the code alone; several had shipped across multiple earlier sessions without anyone catching them until the owner did. Takeaway: a mobile check is not "does this one new thing collapse" - it's worth periodically loading the *whole* page at 375px, section by section, rather than only checking whatever was just touched.
- **Superseded 2026-08-27, do not reintroduce**: an earlier phase of this project built a continuous white-to-cold-blue `main` gradient plus `mesh-echo`/`ambient-halo` decorative overlays meant to make section boundaries invisible. The owner had an external redesign done (ChatGPT, working from a full export of the homepage) that deliberately reverses this: **flat, hard-edged section backgrounds** (each `#section-id` gets one solid/tinted color from `tokens.css`, see the "BOPA V2" override block at the end of `site.css`), no gradients, no halos, no mesh echoes. The rupture between sections is now the point, not something to hide. If a future request asks to "smooth out the transitions between sections" again, flag the contradiction with this rule rather than silently reintroducing gradients - it was tried, it worked, and it was explicitly replaced.
- **Phase/step colors are blue-only, no exceptions**: `--color-step-1/2/3/4` all live inside the BOPA blue family now (cyan → medium blue → deep blue → navy). An earlier version used green/purple/amber for visual variety across pré/per/post-opératoire and médico-économique; the redesign explicitly banned any green, purple, or orange anywhere in the UI. Don't reach for an off-palette accent color for "variety" even when tempted - vary weight, size, or blue shade instead.
- Buttons across the site (`.btn-outline-premium` / `.btn-filled-premium`, used by the Hero, header CTA, and section CTAs) are a **rounded rectangle** (`var(--radius-md)`, ~10px), not a pill - a mid-session mistake gave the header CTA `border-radius: 999px` before the owner corrected it back to match the Hero's actual shape. The base `.btn` class defaults to a pill on its own; every context that uses the premium variants needs its own `border-radius: var(--radius-md)` override (see `.hero-actions .btn`, `.site-header nav > a.btn` in `site.css`) - don't assume the base class's shape is what ships.
- Cards (`.card`) got flatter in the 2026-08-27 redesign too: translucent white (not opaque), a thin 1px border, no drop shadow at rest (a small lift + soft shadow only on hover). Section-level contrast now comes from the flat background colors themselves, not from card depth - don't add shadow/gradient back into cards to make them "pop" against a flat section; that's fighting the new system, not completing it.
- Body paragraphs are **left-aligned**, not justified - this reverses an earlier rule (justify was tried, tuned, and used for months of this project). The 2026-08-27 redesign explicitly calls out justified text as worse for web readability. If any component-specific `text-align: justify`/`hyphens: none` rule surfaces during a future edit, remove it rather than preserve it; the global rule is now `p { text-align: left; line-height: 1.72; }` in the "BOPA V2" block of `site.css`.
- Collapsed accordions (`<details>`) are for secondary/supplementary detail, not for a page's primary content - BOPtech's named projects (InMachina, Salle Hybride) were once the last thing on the page, hidden behind a click. Primary content goes early and stays visible by default.

## Accessibility baseline

- Text over background always passes contrast (4.5:1 for body text).
- Every meaningful image has real alt text.
- The site works with keyboard only; interactive elements are actual links and buttons.
